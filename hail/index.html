The prompt 1125 words

FORMAT
Real handheld first-person storm-chasing video, seen through the eyes of the person filming, as the finished exported video file: the full picture edge to edge, nothing on top of it. The only phone in frame is the one in their own hand; whatever records our picture is never in frame. One continuous take in real time, from one single spot, no cuts. Vertical frame. The video runs at 24 fps. No frame is repeated. Real time, 1x speed, never slow motion.

SCENE
First person, on the shoulder of a straight country highway across flat farmland: someone holds their phone up to film a huge shelf cloud rolling toward them. The cloud's dark front edge passes overhead, the hail starts, and a white curtain of hail sweeps across the fields and over the road: in a few seconds the road, the grass and the fields turn white with hail, as if everything froze. The take ends in the frozen white, hail still falling, the hand and phone still in frame.

REFERENCE IMAGE
Use it for the hand and sleeve, the phone, the road, the shoulder, the fields, the shelf cloud and the light. Everything matches it exactly, zero drift. The movement is described below.

THE PERSON
Nobody is seen. Only the filmer's own right hand, with the black cuff of their hoodie at the wrist, holding a black phone upright, its screen facing us and showing the storm. No head, no shoulder, no second person, no car, no window frame anywhere in the picture.

THE STORM
One huge dark grey shelf cloud, smooth and layered like a rolling wall, its front edge rolling toward us and passing overhead between 3 and 6 s. Under and behind it, a pale white-grey curtain of hail crosses the fields toward the road. The hail: small white pea-sized stones falling fast and straight down; in the air they are thin white streaks, never round balls hanging in the air; on the ground they bounce once or twice and pile up into a white layer. No lightning, no tornado, no funnel, no snowflakes.

LOCATION MAP
We are the person, standing on the paved shoulder, facing along the road. The road runs away ahead and meets the horizon left of centre; the white edge line and the rumble-strip grooves run from the bottom-left corner to the vanishing point. Flat green crop fields fill the right, with a line of dark fence posts 15 m away. The shelf cloud's front edge starts 1 km away and comes toward us until it is overhead.

FIRST FRAME
The hand holds the black phone upright at x 0-25%, y 55-80%, the black cuff at the left edge, the screen showing the grey storm. The rumble-strip grooves and the white edge line run from the bottom-left corner to the vanishing point at x 20%, y 50%. Green fields fill x 30-100%, y 50-68%, the fence posts on the right. The dark shelf cloud fills y 12-50%, its pale lit edge just above the far fields at y 45-50%; lighter grey sky at the top. Instant read: someone filming a giant storm that is about to hit them. No delayed reveal.

CAMERA
First person at standing eye height, held still: only the small natural sway of a person standing. It stays on this one spot for the whole take: it never moves to another place, never tilts up to the sky, never turns around, never cuts, never zooms, never goes to a wide shot, never shows the person. The hand and phone stay in frame from the first frame to the last.

ACTION TIMING
0–3.0 s — The shelf cloud rolls toward us over the fields and grows; the wind bends the crops; the hand holds the phone steady, filming it.
3.0–6.0 s — The cloud's front edge slides up and out of the top of the frame as it passes overhead; the light drops and its dark layered underside fills the sky; under it the white-grey curtain of hail crosses the far fields and the fields behind it fade; the first hailstones hit the asphalt, a few, then dozens, bouncing.
6.0–7.5 s — The curtain reaches the road: hail pours down as thin white streaks, hundreds of stones bouncing on the road and piling into the rumble-strip grooves; stones bounce off the hand and the top of the phone.
7.5–10 s — Everything freezes white: over these seconds the hail covers the asphalt, the rumble strip, the grass and the fields in a solid white layer, and settles white on the sleeve and the top of the phone; the dark fence posts and the white edge line stand out; hail still streaks down; a few drops hit the lens. Hold to the last frame.

PHYSICS AND SCALE
Real storm physics: the shelf cloud moves steadily; the curtain crosses the fields steadily; hailstones fall straight down at real speed and bounce once or twice, never float, never hang in the air; the white layer builds up over seconds, from scattered stones to a solid white cover, the way a real hailstorm whitens a road. The road, the fields and the fence posts stay in the same place the whole time. One storm, one hand, one road. Nothing morphs.

NO FULL-FRAME EVENTS
The hail and the grey air never fill the whole frame: the hand and the phone, the dark fence posts and the white edge line are visible in every frame. The picture never goes white or grey from edge to edge; the white lies on the ground, not across the picture.

LIGHTING
Cool, flat overcast daylight, dropping darker as the cloud passes overhead, then a pale bright white-grey when the ground freezes white; no sun, no lightning flashes.

AUDIO
No music, no score, no narration, no subtitles. Wind rising over the fields; at 4.0 s the first hailstones tick on the asphalt, growing into a loud clatter on the road and on the phone; at 6.0 s the roar of hail all around. Nobody speaks. No words.

LOOK
Real handheld storm-chasing footage, seen as the finished exported file: crisp detail, natural colour, flat overcast contrast, light compression, no colour grade, no film grain, no lens flare, no slow motion, never CGI. No camera app interface on our picture: no shutter button, no gallery thumbnail, no record button, no icons, no timer, no recording dot, no frame lines; the only screen is the phone in the hand.

POSITIVE LOCKS
Nobody in the picture but the filmer's own hand. One phone. One road. One storm. No captions, no subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos, nothing on top of the picture.
ONE HAND. ONE ROAD. ONE STORM. NO CUTS. NO PERSON. NO LIGHTNING. NO TORNADO. NO MUSIC.

Rule 01

One spot, one take

The camera stays on one spot and never turns. Ask for a turn just as the hail arrives and the model cuts to another road with another person.

Rule 02

The cloud moves, not you

To pass overhead, the cloud's front edge slides up and out of the top of the frame. The camera never has to look up.

Rule 03

White on the ground

Everything can freeze white, as long as the white lies on the ground. A frame that goes white edge to edge is where the model cuts.
