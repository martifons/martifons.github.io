He was lost.The hippo gave him a lift.

A duckling sits on a hippo's head while it glides across the river to the bank where his mum is waiting. He hops off. Then his two brothers see the ride and climb on. One still image and one prompt, in one take.

Both prompts, in the order I ran them. Drag the hippo to the bank, gently, to reveal each prompt. Go too fast and the duckling falls in. Tap the river and another duckling swims over for a ride.

The prompt 940 words

FORMAT
Real wildlife video filmed with a long lens from a riverbank, seen as the finished exported video file: the full picture edge to edge, nothing on top of it. Whatever records the picture is never in frame. One continuous take in real time, no cuts. Vertical frame. The video runs at 24 fps. No frame is repeated. Real time, 1x speed, never slow motion.

SCENE
A hippo glides slowly across a calm river with a lost duckling sitting on top of its head, and brings it to the bank where its mother and two siblings are waiting. The hippo lowers its snout to the mud like a ramp; the duckling runs down it to its mother. Then the two other ducklings scramble up the hippo's snout and sit on its head, wanting a ride too, and the hippo wiggles its ears. The hippo is already gliding with the duckling on its head in the first frame. The take ends with two ducklings on the hippo's head and the mother duck with one duckling on the bank.

REFERENCE IMAGE
Use it for the hippo, the ducklings, the mother duck, the water, the bank, the reeds and the light. Everything matches it exactly, zero drift, no smoothing. The movement is described below.

THE HIPPO
One real hippopotamus, only its head above the water the whole take: wet grey-pink skin, small round ears, bulging eyes, wide nostrils, a broad snout. It moves like a real hippo in water: slow, heavy, smooth, pushing a low bow wave; its ears flick, its nostrils open and close. Its body stays under the water; it never stands up, never opens its mouth.

THE DUCKS
One brown speckled mother mallard and three real mallard ducklings, each the size of a fist: fluffy, yellow underneath and dark brown on top, a dark stripe through the eye, tiny dark beaks, orange feet. Ducklings move like real ducklings: quick little runs, wobbly hops, flapping stubby wings, peeping. Exactly three ducklings: one on the hippo at the start, two on the bank.

LOCATION MAP
We are on the bank opposite, 15 m away, at water level, looking across calm brown-green water. A muddy bank is on frame-left, where the mother duck and two ducklings stand at the water's edge. The hippo comes from the middle of the frame toward that bank, moving to frame-left. Green reeds fill the background; reflections in the water below.

FIRST FRAME
The hippo's head at x 45-85%, y 48-62%, facing frame-left, ripples behind it; the duckling on top of its head at x 60-68%, y 42-48%; the muddy bank at x 0-30%, y 50-66% with the mother duck at x 8-20%, y 46-58% and two ducklings beside her at x 18-28%, y 54-60%; the reeds at y 0-40%. Instant read: a duckling riding a hippo toward its mother. No delayed reveal.

CAMERA
Locked off on the opposite bank: a still frame with only the faintest natural movement of a long lens. It never pans, never zooms, never cuts, never moves closer.

ACTION TIMING
0–3.0 s — The hippo glides slowly toward the bank on frame-left, a low ripple ahead of its snout; the duckling sits between its ears, peeping; the mother duck and the two ducklings peep back from the edge of the mud.
3.0–4.5 s — The hippo stops with its snout resting on the mud, half a metre from the mother, and lowers its chin so the top of its snout slopes down to the bank like a ramp.
4.5–6.0 s — The duckling runs down the hippo's snout and hops onto the mud, straight to its mother, who lowers her head and nuzzles it.
6.0–8.0 s — The two other ducklings look at the hippo, then scramble up its wet snout one after the other, slipping a little, and settle on top of its head between its ears.
8.0–10 s — The hippo, with two ducklings on its head, wiggles both ears and blows two small bubbles from its nostrils; the mother duck quacks once. Hold to the last frame.

PHYSICS AND SCALE
Real size: the hippo's head is about 1 m long, each duckling about 10 cm, the mother duck about 55 cm. The hippo moves at a slow walking pace and stops gently; its bow wave is small. The ducklings' feet slip on the wet skin and grip again. One hippo, one mother duck, exactly three ducklings. Nothing morphs.

NO FULL-FRAME EVENTS
Nothing fills the frame: the hippo, the ducks, the bank and the water stay in view in every frame. The splashes are tiny.

LIGHTING
Soft golden morning sun from frame-right, low and warm; gentle highlights on the wet hippo skin and the ducklings' fluff; calm reflections in the water.

AUDIO
No music, no score, no narration, no subtitles. Quiet river sounds: soft lapping water, a low hippo snort, the ducklings' high peeping, the mother's single quack at the end, two small bubble pops. No words.

LOOK
Real wildlife documentary footage shot on a long lens, seen as the finished exported file: shallow depth of field, crisp skin and feather detail, natural colour, no film grain, no lens flare, no slow motion, never CGI, never a cartoon. No camera app interface: no shutter button, no gallery thumbnail, no record button, no icons, no timer, no recording dot, no frame lines.

POSITIVE LOCKS
One hippo, one mother duck, exactly three ducklings. The hippo's body stays under water. No people, no boats. No captions, no subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos, nothing on top of the picture.

Rule 01

Biggest next to smallest

The most dangerous animal in Africa and a duckling the size of a fist. The contrast does the work before anything moves.

Rule 02

Count what's small

Models lose count of little animals first. Say how many there are and where each one is at the start and at the end.

Rule 03

Save the twist for last

The kindness gets them watching; the brothers climbing on gets them sharing. Give the twist its own beat at the end.
