The first frame

The sky is empty on purpose. This image is frame one: the palms, the clubhouse and the street lamps, and one tiny point of flame just off the treeline. Nothing else in the sky yet.

Watch out

Keep the plume out of this image. My first try had the jellyfish already in it, and the video started finished and barely grew.

The prompt 111 words

Real handheld night photo looking up from a sidewalk in a new Florida housing development. In the lower third, young sabal palm trees with wooden support stakes stand silhouetted over a lawn, a wet parking lot and a large two-story clubhouse with hipped roofs, warm windows and tall white street lamps. Above, a wide dark navy night sky with a few faint stars and thin dark clouds. Just above the treeline beyond the clubhouse, on the right, a rocket launch far away: a tiny orange-white point of flame climbing at a steep angle, trailing a thin glowing orange line of exhaust down to the treeline. Night-mode noise, lamps blooming, natural colour.

The prompt 1219 words

FORMAT
Real handheld night video, seen as the finished exported video file: the full picture edge to edge, nothing on top of it. Whatever records the picture is never in frame. One continuous take in real time, no cuts. Vertical frame. The video runs at 24 fps. No frame is repeated. Real time, 1x speed, never slow motion, never a timelapse.

SCENE
Someone standing on a sidewalk in a new Florida housing development at night films a rocket launch far away, from the very beginning: a tiny point of flame climbs out from behind the trees, rises high into the sky and, once it is high enough to catch the sunlight, its exhaust blooms into a strange glowing shape that recalls a giant jellyfish, made only of light and gas. The rocket is already climbing in the first frame, and the sky above it is still empty. The take ends on the glowing shape over the palm trees.

REFERENCE IMAGE
Use it for the palms, the lawn, the parking lot, the clubhouse, the street lamps, the night sky and the rocket's point of flame and its trail. Everything on the ground matches it exactly, zero drift. The sky changes as described below.

THE ROCKET AND ITS PLUME
The rocket is never seen as a body: only a tiny point of light, far away.
1. The climb: an orange-white point of flame that flickers, trailing a thin glowing orange line of exhaust that fades to grey smoke behind it.
2. The puff: high up, where the sunlight still reaches, a small soft puff of exhaust blooms around the point, lit cream and pale blue with a salmon-orange glow at its base, thin and see-through at its edges.
3. The bubble: out of the left side of the puff, a smooth translucent teardrop bubble of pale-blue light swells around the point, which is now brilliant white. It is rounded at its left end and narrows into the puff at its right end, glows from inside, and faint stars show through its edges; faint straight rays of light fan out from the white point to the left. The point keeps crawling slowly to the left and the bubble keeps swelling around it.
The part of the trail high up in the sunlight glows pale violet; the part low near the trees stays dark grey.

LOCATION MAP
The camera is on the sidewalk at the edge of a lawn, at chest height, tilted up about 25 degrees. Lower 38% of the frame: young sabal palm trees with wooden support stakes, their crowns silhouetted against the sky between y 58% and 70%; behind them, a wet parking lot and a large two-story clubhouse with hipped roofs, warm windows and tall white street lamps, along y 68-82%. Upper 60%: the dark navy night sky. The rocket rises from behind the trees beyond the clubhouse, at frame-right, and climbs on a steep curve up and to the left into the upper sky.

FIRST FRAME
Already happening: the point of flame at x 74%, y 50%, just above the treeline beyond the clubhouse, climbing; its thin orange trail running down to the treeline at x 77%, y 60%; the rest of the sky dark navy and empty, a few faint stars; the palm crowns at y 58-70%; the clubhouse and the lamps along the bottom. Instant read: a rocket is going up over a quiet suburb at night. No delayed reveal.

CAMERA
Handheld at chest height, almost still: the small natural sway of a hand holding it up, and a slow tilt up of about 8 degrees during the first 4 seconds, following the point, then it holds. It never zooms, never cuts, never pans down to the ground, never turns to the people. The palm crowns stay in the bottom third from the first frame to the last.

ACTION TIMING
0–3.0 s — The point of flame climbs fast on a steep curve up and to the left, from x 74%, y 50% to x 52%, y 26%, its orange trail stretching behind it down to the trees.
3.0–4.5 s — Up high it catches the sunlight: the flame turns brilliant white, the high part of the trail glows pale violet, and a small soft puff of exhaust blooms around it, cream and pale blue with a salmon glow at its base.
4.5–7.5 s — Out of the left side of the puff, the translucent pale-blue bubble swells around the white point as it crawls slowly left: first milky and small, then glowing blue and wider than the puff, faint rays fanning out from the point.
7.5–10 s — The bubble has grown into a large glowing bell, three times the size of the puff, from x 18% to x 70%, y 12% to 36%, still swelling slowly; the white point keeps crawling left. Hold to the last frame.

PHYSICS AND SCALE
Very far away and enormous: the climb is fast, but the point stays tiny; once it is high, everything moves slowly and smoothly, like a slow sunrise. It only climbs, glows and grows: nothing explodes, nothing falls, nothing flashes. The puff stays small and soft, never a big storm cloud. The palm fronds stir a little in a light breeze. The ground stays still. Nothing morphs: one point of flame, one trail, one puff, one bubble.

NO FULL-FRAME EVENTS
The plume never fills the frame and never lights up the whole sky: the sky around it stays dark navy in every frame, and the palms and the lamps stay visible from the first frame to the last.

LIGHTING
Night on the ground: warm and white street lamps, the clubhouse windows, the wet parking lot reflecting them, the palms almost black against the sky. The rocket's flame is a small orange point and never lights the ground. High above, the plume is lit by sunlight from below the horizon: glowing icy blue and cream, brighter than anything on the ground, with a salmon-orange glow at the base of the puff.

AUDIO
No music, no score, no narration, no subtitles. A Florida night: crickets and tree frogs, a distant highway hum. From 2 s, a deep distant rumble like faraway thunder, rolling and steady: the launch heard from many miles away. Exactly two spoken lines, quiet, from behind the camera: at 5 s a man whispers "What... is that?"; at 8 s a woman says, half laughing and half scared, "Bro, is this the end of the world?". Nobody else speaks.

LOOK
Real handheld night footage, seen as the finished exported file: night-mode noise in the dark sky, street lamps blooming, slight softness, natural colour, no colour grade, no film grain, no lens flare, no cinematic look. No camera app interface: no shutter button, no gallery thumbnail, no record button, no icons, no timer, no recording dot, no frame lines.

POSITIVE LOCKS
It is a rocket launch and its exhaust plume and nothing else: not a UFO, not a spaceship, not a comet, not a portal, not an explosion, not fireworks, not an aurora, not a storm cloud, no tentacles. No visible rocket body. No captions, no subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos, nothing on top of the picture.
ONE ROCKET. ONE PLUME. ONE SKY. NO CUTS. NO INTERFACE. NO SLOW MOTION. NO MUSIC.

Rule 01

Leave the sky empty

Whatever is in your first frame is already done. To watch something form, the image only gets its cause: a rocket just off the ground.

Rule 02

Give the final size

Say where it ends up in the frame, like x 18 to 70%. Just saying it grows gets you a shape that barely moves.

Rule 03

Close the easy doors

Light and gas, not a UFO, not a spaceship, not a comet, not a storm cloud. Name what the model would pick and rule it out.
